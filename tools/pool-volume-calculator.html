Why pool volume matters

Every chemical dose is calculated per gallon. Add 20% too little chlorine because you underestimated your pool size and you'll be chasing algae all summer. Add 20% too much acid and you'll overshoot your pH target every time. Volume is the one number that makes every other calculation correct — or wrong.

How to measure accurately

For length and width, a tape measure along the water line is the most accurate method. For depth, use a telescoping pole or measuring rod. Measure at the shallow end, the deep end, and two or three spots in between. For a pool with a sloped bottom, average depth = (shallow depth + deep depth) ÷ 2, which is what the "Sloped" option above calculates.

For unusual shapes, the approach is always the same: break the pool into sections you can measure, calculate each section, and add them. An L-shaped pool is two rectangles. A kidney pool has two lobes — the (A + B) formula approximates the curved boundary.

Volume is always an estimate

Pool walls aren't perfectly vertical, floors aren't perfectly flat, and tape measures aren't perfectly level. A well-measured pool still has ±5–10% error. Dose chemicals conservatively — you can always add more, but you can't easily remove what's already in.

The constant 7.48 converts cubic feet to US gallons. The oval and round constant 5.9 is π/4 × 7.48 ≈ 5.87, rounded to 5.9. The kidney constant 0.45 accounts for the indented-lobe geometry and is consistent with the Pool & Hot Tub Alliance standard.

Worked example: rectangular pool

A standard backyard pool measures 40 ft × 20 ft with a sloped bottom — 3.5 ft at the shallow end, 8 ft at the deep end. Average depth = (3.5 + 8) ÷ 2 = 5.75 ft.

Volume = 40 × 20 × 5.75 × 7.48 = ~34,400 gallons. A 20% underestimate (28,000 gallons) would leave you chronically under-dosing chlorine; a 20% overestimate means you're overdosing every treatment. Measure carefully and round down slightly when in doubt.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find out how many gallons my pool holds?

Measure length, width, and depth, then use the formula for your pool's shape. The calculator above handles rectangle, oval, round, kidney, and L-shaped pools. For unusual shapes, break the pool into sections you can measure separately, calculate each section, and add them.

What if I don't know my pool's exact depth?

Use a telescoping pole or weighted string at the shallow end, deep end, and two points in between. For a sloped bottom, average depth = (shallow + deep) ÷ 2 — which is what the "Sloped" option above calculates. A single depth measurement taken at the midpoint is good enough if that's all you have.

How accurate is this pool volume calculator?

Within ±5–10% for careful measurements. Pool walls aren't perfectly vertical and floors aren't flat. That's accurate enough for chemical dosing — just dose toward the conservative end of your target range and retest before adding more rather than adding everything at once.

Should I include the spa in my pool volume?

Yes, if the spa shares water with the pool — most attached spas do. Use the "Include Spa" add-on above. If your spa is freestanding with a separate pump and water, calculate and treat it independently.

What pool volume do I use for chemical calculations?

Use this calculated gallons figure for every dose — chlorine, muriatic acid, baking soda, and calcium chloride all scale with water volume. An incorrect volume is the most common cause of consistent over- or under-dosing across every chemical you add.
