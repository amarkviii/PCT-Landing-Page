What is LSI?

The Langelier Saturation Index measures whether your pool water is balanced, corrosive, or scale-forming. It combines pH, temperature, alkalinity, calcium hardness, and TDS into a single number.

LSI near 0.0 — Balanced water. Won't damage surfaces or equipment.

LSI below −0.3 — Corrosive. Can etch plaster, dissolve grout, and damage concrete and tile surfaces.

LSI above +0.3 — Scale-forming. Calcium deposits build up on tile, heater elements, and salt cells.

Your pool surface sets your recommended target LSI band — a tighter, more conservative window inside the broad −0.3 to +0.3 balanced zone — with a small downward shift in hot, arid climates where evaporation naturally pushes LSI up. Always confirm actual balance with the full calculation: pH, total alkalinity, calcium hardness, water temperature, and salt/TDS.

How to Use PoolChem ▸
⚙️
1. Set Up Your Pool
Go to Settings and enter your pool volume, surface type, and climate zone. Your surface (plaster, pebble, vinyl, or fiberglass) sets your LSI water-balance targets (and the calcium range for vinyl and fiberglass). If you have a salt water generator (SWG), enter the brand — this enables salt-specific recommendations and adjusts your FC target (SWG pools run a lower minimum FC: 5% of CYA, vs. 7.5% for non-SWG). Accurate settings directly affect dosing amounts and ideal ranges. Set up your water-test, inventory, and equipment-maintenance reminders on the Tasks tab.
🧪
2. Test Your Water
Use a test kit or take a sample to your local pool store. Test at least once a week, or after heavy rain, parties, or chemical additions.
📝
3. Log Your Reading
Tap + Log Reading and enter your test results. You don't need every field — just enter what you have. Not every parameter needs to be tested every time:
Weekly: Free Chlorine, pH, Total Alkalinity
Monthly: CYA, Calcium Hardness, Salt
As needed: Phosphates, Iron, Copper
At minimum, Free Chlorine and pH will give you a health score and recommendations.
Chlorine fields: Enter FC plus either TC (from a pool store report) or CC (from a DPD drop test) — the app calculates the other automatically. If your DPD test shows no CC, enter 0.
Better recommendations: Adding CYA along with FC gives you an accurate chlorine target range. Entering both pH and TA together ensures the correct treatment order. The app will remind you if these are missing when you save.
Log often: The more readings you log, the better the app can detect trends, spot patterns, and refine your health score. Weekly testing is ideal during pool season.
📊
4. Check Your Dashboard
After logging, the Dashboard shows your pool's health score, key parameter status, and any time-sensitive alerts.
✅
5. Follow "Do This Now"
The app prioritizes what to fix first. Make one adjustment at a time, wait 4–6 hours, then retest before making the next change.
📈
6. Track Over Time
Use the History tab to see charts, spot trends, and understand how your pool changes week to week. Export your data regularly as a backup.
Pro Tip: Upgrade to Pro for score breakdowns, trend analysis, climate-specific ranges, a health score simulator, and more.
How to Test Your Water ▸
Use one of the methods below to get your water test results, then enter them on the Log tab:
🏪 Pool Store Test
Bring a water sample to a local pool supply store for a free analysis. Convenient for a full set of readings, but accuracy can vary depending on testing methods and calibration.
🧪 FAS-DPD Liquid Test Kit (e.g., Taylor K-2006)
The gold standard for home testing. Uses a drop titration method for highly accurate chlorine measurement, along with pH and total alkalinity (TA). Ideal for regular monitoring.
🧂 Salt Test Kit (e.g., Taylor K-1766)
Uses a titration method to measure chloride, which is used to calculate salt level. More accurate than test strips and recommended for pools with saltwater chlorine generators.
💧 Liquid Color-Match Test Kit (e.g., 4-way or 5-way kits)
Uses liquid reagents with a color chart to estimate chlorine, pH, and other parameters. More reliable than test strips, but less precise than FAS-DPD methods — especially at higher chlorine levels.
💊 DPD Color-Match Test Kit
Uses DPD reagents (liquid or tablets) with a color chart to estimate chlorine levels. Reliable for routine use, but less precise than FAS-DPD methods — especially at higher chlorine levels.
📋 Test Strips
Fast and convenient, but less precise and more sensitive to storage conditions. Best for quick checks between more thorough tests.
Tips for Accurate Results
• Collect water from elbow depth, away from return jets
• Test at the same time each day for more consistent trending
How to Use Inventory ▸
📦
Add a Chemical
Under Add / Update Chemical, pick an item from the dropdown — or choose Enter custom name for anything not listed. Enter how much you have on hand (free-form, e.g. "2 gallons" or "3 tablets"), then tap + Add to Inventory.
🔄
Add or Update in One Step
Saving a chemical that's already on your list updates that entry instead of creating a duplicate, and stamps the current date so you can see when you last changed it. Tap the ✕ on any row to remove it.
📝
Add Notes
Use the Last Added / Notes field to record when you last purchased or added a chemical — e.g. "4/15 — added 1 gal" — helpful for tracking usage over time.
🔔
Set a Reminder
Enable the Inventory Check reminder on the Tasks tab to get periodic nudges to review your stock. The reminder resets each time you update your inventory.
Importing & Exporting Data ▸
⬇️
Export
Tap Export CSV on the History tab to save a backup of all your readings. Export regularly — your data is stored on-device only.
⬆️
Import
Tap Import CSV on the History tab to load readings from a .csv file. The app can re-import its own exports, or accept a spreadsheet with column headers.
📋
CSV Format
Two layouts are supported:
Column layout — Header row with columns like Date, FC, TC, pH, TA, CH, CYA, Salt, Temp, Phosphates, Iron, Copper, Notes. Only Date is required — other columns are optional.
Spreadsheet layout — Dates across the top row with parameters down the first column (the format used by many pool spreadsheets). Extra columns (like "Range") are ignored.
Recognized labels — The app matches common names: Free Chlorine, Total Chlorine, pH, Total Alkalinity, Alkalinity, Calcium Hardness, CYA, Stabilizer, Cyanuric Acid, Salt, Phosphates, Iron, Copper, Water Temp, Temperature, Notes. Calculation rows (like Temp Factor or LSI) are ignored.
Dates can be M/D/YYYY, M/D/YY, or YYYY-MM-DD.
🔄
Duplicates
Readings with a date that already exists in your history will be skipped, so it's safe to re-import the same file.
