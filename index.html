Why is total chlorine higher than free chlorine?

That gap is combined chlorine (chloramines) — used-up chlorine that's no longer sanitizing. Above 0.5 ppm means it's time to shock.

Why is my free chlorine low?

Usually a CYA imbalance, sun exposure, high swimmer load, or algae consuming it. Check your CYA level first — without it, chlorine won't hold.

Why is my pool water cloudy?

Five common causes: low chlorine, high pH, high alkalinity, poor filtration, or high calcium hardness. Start by testing FC and pH.

Why does my pH keep going up?

High alkalinity, SWG electrolysis, aeration, and fresh plaster all push pH up. Lower it with muriatic acid — but fix the root cause too.

Does liquid chlorine raise CYA?

No. Only stabilized chlorine (trichlor tablets, dichlor granules) adds CYA. Liquid chlorine, bleach, and cal-hypo do not.

Why is my pool cloudy after shocking?

Usually dead algae, not enough chlorine to finish the job, high pH, or cal-hypo residue. Keep filtering and maintain shock level.

Pool chemistry FAQ

What should free chlorine be in a pool?

The ideal free chlorine (FC) level depends on your cyanuric acid (CYA). At CYA 30, aim for 2–4 ppm. At CYA 50, aim for 4–6 ppm. The rule of thumb is FC should be at least 7.5% of your CYA level. See the full FC/CYA chart →

What should total chlorine be?

Total chlorine (TC) should be very close to your free chlorine reading. The difference between TC and FC is combined chlorine (chloramines). If TC is more than 0.5 ppm above FC, it's time to shock. Free chlorine vs total chlorine explained →

Can total chlorine be lower than free chlorine?

No — total chlorine is free chlorine plus combined chlorine, so TC should always be equal to or higher than FC. If your test shows TC below FC, the reading is a testing error. Try retesting with fresh reagents or a different kit. Learn more about chlorine types →

Why is my pool chlorine high but still cloudy?

High chlorine with cloudy water usually means high CYA is locking up your chlorine, or high pH/alkalinity is causing minerals to precipitate. Your FC reading looks fine, but the effective chlorine may be too low. Check CYA first. Diagnose cloudy pool water →

Does shocking lower combined chlorine?

Yes — that's exactly what shocking does. By raising FC high enough to reach breakpoint chlorination (roughly 10x your combined chlorine level), you destroy chloramines and reset CC to zero. Use liquid chlorine for the cleanest shock. What to do after shocking →

How often should I test free chlorine?

2–3 times per week during swim season, weekly in the off-season. Test in the morning for the most accurate baseline — UV breaks down chlorine throughout the day. Always test after heavy rain, pool parties, or adding chemicals. Full testing schedule →

What is the ideal pool pH?

Keep pH between 7.4 and 7.8, with 7.5–7.6 being the sweet spot. Below 7.2, water becomes corrosive and irritates skin and eyes. Above 7.8, chlorine loses effectiveness and calcium can precipitate out, causing cloudy water and scale. Full pH guide →

How much chlorine should I add to my pool?

It depends on your pool volume, current FC level, and target FC (which is set by your CYA). As a rough guide, 1 oz of liquid chlorine (12.5%) raises FC by about 1 ppm per 1,000 gallons. PoolChem Tracker calculates the exact dose for you. Chlorine dosing guide →
