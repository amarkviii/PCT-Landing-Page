Pool chemistry FAQ

What should free chlorine be in a pool?

The ideal FC depends on your CYA. At CYA 30, aim for 2–4 ppm. At CYA 50, aim for 4–6 ppm. The rule of thumb: FC should be at least 7.5% of your CYA. See the FC/CYA chart →

Why is my pool cloudy even though chlorine is high?

Usually high CYA is locking up your chlorine, or high pH/alkalinity is causing precipitation. FC looks fine, but effective chlorine is too low. Check CYA first. Diagnose cloudy water →

What is the ideal pool pH?

Keep pH between 7.4 and 7.8, with 7.5–7.6 as the sweet spot. Below 7.2 the water becomes corrosive. Above 7.8, chlorine loses most of its effectiveness. Full pH guide →

How much chlorine should I add?

It depends on your pool volume, current FC, and your CYA-based target. As a rough guide, 1 oz of 12.5% liquid raises FC by ~1 ppm per 1,000 gallons. Use the chlorine dose calculator →

What is LSI?

The Langelier Saturation Index tells you whether your water is balanced, corrosive, or scaling — even when pH looks fine. It combines pH, calcium, alkalinity, and temperature. LSI explained →

How often should I test my pool?

2–3 times per week during swim season, weekly in the off-season. Test in the morning — UV burns off chlorine throughout the day. Always retest after rain, a pool party, or adding chemicals. Testing schedule →
