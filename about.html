Why I built PoolChem Tracker

In 2022, my family built a house and then built an inground saltwater pool from scratch.

When we filled it for the first time, I knew almost nothing about pool chemistry. Like most new pool owners, I relied on the pool store and a handful of apps. The advice often conflicted, the recommendations didn't seem to solve the problems I was having, and I still didn't understand why my water kept getting out of balance.

I am not a chemist by training. My background is in business, and I am the kind of person who turns everything into a spreadsheet. So from the very first week, I started logging every water test, every chemical addition, and every recommendation from the pool store.

After a few months, patterns started to emerge and they didn't match what I was being told.

Eventually, I figured out what was really happening. I live in the California desert, where triple-digit temperatures, intense sun, rapid evaporation, and extremely hard fill water all have a major impact on pool chemistry. The problems I was fighting, rising stabilizer (CYA), heavy chlorine demand, and scale buildup, were not random. They were a direct result of my environment.

The pool store's advice did not account for that. Neither did any of the apps I tried.

Scale turned out to be one of the biggest challenges. Understanding it led me down a rabbit hole into the Langelier Saturation Index (LSI), a calculation that predicts whether water is balanced, corrosive, or likely to form scale. It quickly became clear that this was one of the most important measurements for keeping water healthy and one that many pool apps either ignored or oversimplified.

I decided there had to be a better way.

So I built one.

That original spreadsheet eventually became PoolChem Tracker. I wanted a tool that tracked water chemistry over time, spotted trends before they became expensive problems, and gave recommendations based on my actual pool, my actual climate, and the way pools behave in the real world, not generic averages.

How the calculator works

PoolChem Tracker calculates LSI from the measurements you actually test: pH, calcium hardness, total alkalinity, water temperature, and total dissolved solids (TDS).

Some calculators let you adjust individual values until you reach a "perfect" LSI. That is not how LSI is intended to be used.

The correct approach is to test your water first, then calculate LSI from those real measurements. The Pool & Hot Tub Alliance has specifically warned against manipulating inputs simply to reach a desired LSI, noting that doing so is inconsistent with the ANSI/APSP/ICC-11 water chemistry standard.

That is the approach PoolChem Tracker follows.
