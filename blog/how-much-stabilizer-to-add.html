How much stabilizer to add to a pool

5 min read · August 2026 · By Jeffrey Thompson
About 13 oz of granular stabilizer per 10,000 gallons raises CYA by 10 ppm. Calculate your exact dose with the formula below, add it pre-dissolved in one shot, and wait 24–48 hours before retesting. CYA doesn’t dissipate — don’t overdose.

The formula

Dose formula oz to add = (target CYA increase in ppm × pool gallons) ÷ 7,500

Example: 20,000-gallon pool, current CYA 10 ppm, target 40 ppm. Increase needed = 30 ppm.

(30 × 20,000) ÷ 7,500 = 80 oz (5 lbs)

Always base the dose on your current CYA reading, not an assumption. If you haven’t tested CYA recently, test it first — adding stabilizer to a pool that already has 40 ppm would push it to 70+ ppm, which creates a different problem. See high CYA in a pool for why that matters.

Don’t overdose. CYA accumulates permanently — it can only be removed by draining water. If you add too much, the only fix is a partial drain. Add the calculated amount, wait 48 hours, and retest before adding more.

How to add stabilizer correctly

  1. Measure the dose. Calculate using the formula above based on your current CYA reading and target. For most pools starting from zero or near-zero, one 4–5 lb bag is the right order of magnitude.
  2. Pre-dissolve in warm water. Fill a clean 5-gallon bucket about two-thirds full with warm tap water. Slowly pour in the granular stabilizer and stir. It dissolves slowly — give it 5–10 minutes and stir periodically. The solution will be cloudy; that’s normal.
  3. Pour near a return jet. With the pump running, slowly pour the dissolved solution directly into the pool near a return jet. This distributes it into the circulation. Don’t dump it in one spot.
  4. Run the pump continuously. Keep the pump running for at least 24 hours to circulate and distribute the CYA through the entire pool volume.
  5. Wait 24–48 hours before retesting. CYA takes time to fully dissolve and distribute. A retest at 24 hours often reads lower than the final value because granules are still dissolving. If the 24-hour reading seems low, test again at 48 hours before adding more.

Never add stabilizer to the skimmer basket

Some guides suggest adding granular stabilizer to the skimmer and letting pool water dissolve it. This is risky: undissolved granules can clog the basket, the skimmer neck, or the pump basket, and creates a high-concentration slug of CYA that doesn’t distribute evenly. Always pre-dissolve in a bucket first.

What product to buy

Pool stabilizer, pool conditioner, and cyanuric acid are all the same chemical — granular cyanuric acid (CYA). The name on the bag doesn’t matter. Buy the largest bag that fits your dose calculation; the per-ounce cost drops significantly in bulk. A 4 lb bag runs about $10–15 at pool supply stores and big-box retailers in season.

Dichlor granules and trichlor tablets also add CYA as a byproduct of chlorination — but these are chlorine products, not stabilizer products. If you’re adding CYA intentionally, use pure granular cyanuric acid so you control exactly how much you’re adding. See which chlorine products add CYA for a full breakdown.

How often do you need to add stabilizer?

If you’re using liquid chlorine (sodium hypochlorite) as your primary sanitizer: add stabilizer once at the start of the season to reach 30–50 ppm, then test CYA monthly. CYA drifts downward slowly from dilution — splash-out, backwashing, and rain — typically 5–10 ppm over a full summer. A small top-up of 13–26 oz per 10,000 gallons mid-season is common.

If you’re using trichlor tablets as your primary sanitizer: you probably don’t need to add stabilizer at all. Trichlor adds significant CYA with every dose — the bigger risk is CYA climbing too high over the season, not too low. Test CYA monthly and watch for it creeping toward 70–80 ppm. See high CYA in a pool if it does.

Fresh fill or major drain: start from scratch

After a full drain and refill, or opening a new pool, CYA starts at zero. Use the dose chart above based on your pool volume and target (30–50 ppm for a regular pool, 60–80 ppm for a saltwater generator pool). Don’t rely on any residual CYA — test first, then dose.

Frequently asked questions

How much pool stabilizer do I add?

oz to add = (target CYA increase × pool gallons) ÷ 7,500. For a 15,000-gallon pool going from CYA 10 to 40 ppm (+30 ppm), that’s 60 oz (3.75 lbs). Use the dose chart above for common pool sizes.

How long does pool stabilizer take to work?

24–48 hours to fully dissolve and distribute. Run the pump continuously during this period. Don’t retest and add more at 12 hours — wait a full 48 hours for the most accurate reading before deciding if a top-up is needed.

Can you add too much stabilizer?

Yes, and it’s a real risk because CYA can only be removed by draining. If CYA is already at 30–50 ppm, don’t add more. If you accidentally overdose, a partial drain is the only fix. Calculate based on your current CYA and target, not a rule of thumb.

Where do you add stabilizer in a pool?

Pre-dissolve in a bucket of warm water, then pour slowly near a return jet with the pump running. Never add to the skimmer basket — it can clog and doesn’t distribute evenly.

How often do you add stabilizer?

For liquid chlorine pools: once at season start to reach 30–50 ppm, then a small top-up mid-season if CYA drifts below 30 ppm. For trichlor tablet pools: usually never — tablets add CYA continuously and the risk is accumulation, not depletion.
