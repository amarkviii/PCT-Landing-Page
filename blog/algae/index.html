Pool algae guides

Algae in a pool always has the same root cause: free chlorine dropped below the minimum for your CYA level. The fix is shock-level FC, daily brushing, and sustained treatment until the overnight chlorine loss test passes. What varies is the algae type — green algae, mustard algae, and black algae each need a different approach, and recurring algae almost always has a systemic cause you haven't fixed yet.

The complete algae treatment guide: identify the type, shock to CYA × 40%, brush daily, run the pump 24/7, and hold until the OCLT passes.

Pool Turned Green Overnight? Emergency Triage

A pool that turns green overnight has a fresh algae bloom from FC dropping below the CYA minimum. Triage in 5 minutes, then shock and hold to clear in 3–7 days.

Mustard Algae vs. Green Algae

Yellow-brown slime that brushes off but keeps coming back? That's mustard algae — chlorine-resistant, and it returns until you wash all swimsuits and decontaminate gear.

7 minRead guide →

Black Algae in a Pool

Black algae (cyanobacteria) forms a hard shell and roots into plaster. Regular shocking doesn't work — it requires wire brushing before every chlorine dose for 1–2 weeks.

7 minRead guide →

Why Algae Keeps Coming Back

Recurring algae has a root cause: FC below the CYA minimum, high CYA, incomplete treatment, contaminated equipment, or circulation dead spots. Find the pattern by tracking readings.

7 minRead guide →

Dead Algae but Pool Still Cloudy?

Killed the algae but water is still hazy? Run filtration continuously, use clarifier or floc, and pass the overnight chlorine loss test before stopping treatment.

5 minRead guide →

Green Water but Chlorine Is High?

High chlorine with a green pool usually means CYA is above 80 ppm — the stabilizer has locked up most of the chlorine's active fraction. The fix is a partial drain.
